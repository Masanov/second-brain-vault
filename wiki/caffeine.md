# Caffeine

An adenosine receptor antagonist. It does not create alertness; it blocks the signal that
would otherwise be read as tiredness. See [[sleep-pressure]] for what that signal is and
why blocking it defers rather than removes it.

## Pharmacokinetics

Half-life in healthy adults averages **5–6 hours**, with an individual range of roughly
2–8 hours. Liver enzyme activity, oral contraceptive use, pregnancy, and smoking all shift
it.

Half-life is the wrong number for practical decisions. After one half-life, half the dose
is still circulating. Working from the **quarter-life of 10–12 hours** gives a better
answer to "will this coffee reach my night".

| Dose 200 mg at 16:00 | Remaining |
|---|---|
| 22:00 | ~100 mg |
| 04:00 | ~50 mg |

## Tolerance

Tolerance to the subjective alertness effect develops within days. Tolerance to the
sleep-disrupting effect does not develop to the same degree. Self-report is therefore
unreliable: people who say caffeine does not affect their sleep still show measurable
reductions in slow-wave sleep under polysomnography.

## Timing of the first coffee — disputed

A widely repeated recommendation delays the first coffee by 90–120 minutes after waking,
on the grounds that drinking immediately blunts the cortisol awakening response.

Recorded here as an **open question**. The cortisol awakening response is well documented;
the step from that observation to the specific timing figure appears to be inference rather
than a controlled finding, and the source reports being unable to locate a trial
establishing it. Left unresolved rather than decided — see the conflicting-information rule
in `AGENTS.md`.
